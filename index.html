When Hiring Becomes Agent-Mediated: Evaluating Access and Recurrence in Two-Agent Résumé Screening

If an employer agent and a candidate agent screen a résumé together, does the same person get through?

Jian Gao1, Hang Jiang1,2,3

  • N1Northeastern University
  • 2MIT
  • 3Harvard University
REALM Workshop @ EMNLP 2026, Budapest Archival short paper
Paper Poster

tl;dr Résumé screening is the first hiring gate, and LLM screening is usually one model call. As employers and candidates adopt agents, it can become an exchange between an employer agent and a candidate agent. We compare both procedures on 600 constructed résumé–job pairs with GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.7.

Finding The two procedures advance similar numbers of applications, but not the same applications, and some selections specific to one procedure do not recur when the pipeline is re-run. Results ↓

    Finding 2  Membership

    Similar volume, different applications

    .000

    Jaccard

    Applications passing all 3 runs on the 191-pair borderline pool, with one-call scores re-read at the cutoff closest to two-agent pass volume (.590 GPT-5.5, .575 Opus 4.7). “Only” = 3/3 under one procedure, fewer under the other. No cutoff on the one-call mean reproduces the two-agent set: the best Jaccard over all cutoffs is .436 and .361. Run by run, every within-procedure rerun pair overlaps more than every cross-procedure pair.

    Finding 3  Recurrence

    Lower recurrence among two-agent-only selections

    Fresh re-runs: share passing 3/3 again under the originating procedure

    Pooled application–model cases; pooled shares are descriptive because 26 pair IDs appear under both models. Two-agent-only vs. shared, per model: GPT-5.5 24/24 vs. 7/12 (Fisher p = .0021); Opus 4.7 10/12 vs. 4/8 (p = .161, uncertain).

    Caveat Overall recurrence is similar: 45/56 (80.4%) two-agent vs. 60/71 (84.5%) one-call. The selected groups may differ in difficulty, so this is not evidence that two-agent screening is less reliable in general.

    Scope

    Constructed data

    Constructed résumés and public LinkedIn postings; no real applicants or hiring outcomes. GPT-5.5 also generated the résumés.

    Whole procedures

    We compare complete procedures and do not attribute differences to dialogue, role separation, compute, gating or rules.

    No accuracy claim

    We make no claim about accuracy or fairness.
